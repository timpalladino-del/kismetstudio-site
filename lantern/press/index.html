The idea

Most households already own a week of food. What they don't have is a list.

The last ten years have put most American households through at least one long outage. Lantern is for the ordinary version of being ready: a full pantry, water that gets rotated, medicine that hasn't expired, and a plan everyone in the house knows. It keeps the list, dates it, and turns it into days of cover for the actual people in the house. Real calories and real gallons against the Red Cross and CDC targets, not a score.

"The idea came from standing in the dark with a flashlight, knowing there was a week of food in the house and not being able to say where. Being ready for the next one shouldn't feel like joining a movement. It should feel like a tidy pantry."
Tim Palladino, Kismet Studio

What it does

Seven things, in one sentence each.

Know what you have

Barcode and printed-date scanning on the device, a shelf-life library for more than ninety staples with a source on every entry, and nutrition facts pulled in from the barcode.

Readiness in days

Calories per person follow Mifflin-St Jeor when weight and height are known, otherwise the Dietary Guidelines bands. Water follows the CDC gallon a day. Pets count too.

A medication card

Dose, times, take with food, who prescribed it, the pharmacy number. One card for the whole house to share as text or print for the fridge and the go-bag.

Profiles by link

Family members fill in their own details from an invitation and send them back. No account, no server; the link is the transport.

Outage mode

When the power goes, start the clocks on the fridge and freezer, open the generator and carbon monoxide cards, set a check-in reminder.

Free for everyone, always

The toolkit

SOS strobe and flashlight, whistle, compass, NOAA Weather Radio frequencies, emergency numbers, a meeting plan, and know-how cards from the CDC, USDA, FEMA and the Red Cross.

About Kismet Studio

Small, careful apps for iPhone.

Kismet Studio makes apps from Florida: CastleWise, the owner's manual your house never came with; Sumhow, a daily calculator puzzle; and Lantern. Each one is built around a single idea, keeps its data on the phone, and is written to be useful on the first day.

Lantern gives general information from public sources for planning. It is not medical or safety advice. In an emergency, call 911 first.
